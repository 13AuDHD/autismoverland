Accessibility

Access Shouldn't Be an Afterthought

Last updated: August 13, 2026

Llama Scout was built around the idea that knowing more about a place before you arrive can remove unnecessary barriers. That applies to the website too.

Our Commitment

Llama Scout is committed to making its website, information, maps, field reports, and other digital resources usable by as many people as reasonably possible, including people with disabilities and neurodivergent users.

Accessibility is considered part of the design and development process rather than a separate feature added afterward.

We continue to review and improve the website as Llama Scout grows.

Accessibility Standard

Llama Scout uses the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines as a technical reference for improving digital accessibility.

Our current goal is to work toward conformance with WCAG 2.2 Level AA.

WCAG is developed by the World Wide Web Consortium and provides guidance for making web content more accessible to people with visual, auditory, physical, speech, cognitive, learning, language, and neurological disabilities.

Accessibility is an ongoing process, and stating this goal does not mean every part of Llama Scout has already been independently audited or certified as fully conforming.

What We Try to Make Accessible

Llama Scout aims to support accessibility through practices such as:

  • Readable text with sufficient contrast
  • Responsive layouts that work across screen sizes
  • Keyboard-accessible navigation and controls
  • Visible keyboard focus states
  • Semantic headings and page structure
  • Descriptive alternative text for meaningful images
  • Labels for forms and interactive controls
  • Clear link and button text
  • Interfaces that do not rely on color alone to communicate meaning
  • Content that can be enlarged without becoming unusable
  • Reduced unnecessary visual clutter
  • Predictable navigation and page structure
  • Touch targets that are practical on mobile devices

These practices reflect common accessibility principles identified in WCAG and federal accessibility guidance. [oai_citation:1‡ADA.gov](https://www.ada.gov/resources/web-guidance/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Neurodivergent Accessibility

Llama Scout also considers accessibility needs that may not always be captured by traditional physical-accessibility checklists.

We try to make the website easier to use for people who may experience sensory overload, attention differences, executive-function challenges, cognitive fatigue, or difficulty processing dense or unpredictable interfaces.

This includes an emphasis on:

  • Clear and predictable navigation
  • Plain-language descriptions
  • Consistent page layouts
  • Breaking large amounts of information into sections
  • Reducing unnecessary animation and visual distraction
  • Providing detailed information before someone has to make a decision

Accessibility Information About Outdoor Places

Llama Scout includes accessibility observations in Scout Reports because information about terrain and facilities can determine whether a place is usable for someone.

Depending on the location, a Scout Report may include information about:

  • Wheelchair suitability
  • Outdoor mobility-device suitability
  • Step-free access from the vehicle
  • Walking surfaces
  • Distance from parking
  • Accessible toilets
  • Accessible picnic tables
  • Terrain and slope
  • Vehicle access
  • Nearby obstacles

These observations are intended to provide useful context, but they are not formal ADA accessibility inspections or certifications.

Outdoor conditions can also change after a place is personally scouted.

See the Outdoor Disclaimer for more information.

Maps and Interactive Features

Interactive maps can present accessibility challenges, particularly for keyboard users and people using screen readers.

Llama Scout aims to provide important place information outside the map itself through individual Scout Reports and the Places directory so that access to core information does not depend entirely on interacting with a visual map.

Images

Meaningful images should include alternative text that describes the important visual information they contain.

Decorative images may intentionally use empty alternative text where appropriate so screen readers do not announce unnecessary visual elements.

Third-Party Content

Some parts of Llama Scout may rely on third-party services, libraries, maps, embedded content, or external websites.

We cannot guarantee the accessibility of services that we do not control, but we try to avoid making critical Llama Scout information available only through inaccessible third-party tools.

Known Limitations

Llama Scout is actively developed, and accessibility issues may exist despite efforts to prevent them.

Areas that may require continued review include:

  • Interactive maps
  • Third-party map controls
  • Complex filters and forms
  • Older content created before current accessibility practices were adopted
  • Third-party embedded services

These areas will continue to be reviewed as the site changes.

Need Help Accessing Something?

If you cannot access information, complete an action, use a feature, or understand part of Llama Scout because of an accessibility barrier, please let us know.

When reporting an accessibility problem, it is helpful to include:

  • The page or feature you were trying to use
  • What happened
  • The device or browser you were using, if relevant
  • Any assistive technology involved, if you are comfortable sharing it
  • What would make the information or feature usable for you

You do not need to provide information about your disability in order to report an accessibility problem.

Alternative Access

If information on Llama Scout is inaccessible to you, we will make reasonable efforts to provide the same information in another usable format when possible.

Ongoing Improvement

Accessibility standards, technology, and Llama Scout itself will continue to change.

We expect this accessibility statement and the website's implementation to evolve with them.

Contact

Accessibility questions, problems, or suggestions may be submitted through the contact information provided on Llama Scout.

Accessibility feedback is useful. If something does not work, we would rather know about it than assume everything is fine.
