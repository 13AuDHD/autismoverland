Know Before You Go

Outdoor Disclaimer

Last updated: August 13, 2026

Llama Scout is designed to help you arrive better informed, not to replace current conditions, official information, personal judgment, or common sense.

Conditions Can Change

Outdoor places do not stay the same.

Roads can wash out, trees can fall, gates can close, campsites can disappear, trails can erode, rivers can rise, snow and mud can make roads impassable, and land-management rules can change after a location was last scouted.

A Llama Scout report describes conditions that were observed or known at a particular point in time. It is not a guarantee that those same conditions exist when you arrive.

Check Current Information

Before traveling, check current information from appropriate official sources whenever it could affect your safety, access, or legal use of an area.

This may include:

  • Weather forecasts and severe-weather alerts
  • Road and trail conditions
  • Seasonal closures
  • Wildfire activity and fire restrictions
  • Flooding or monsoon conditions
  • Permits and fees
  • Camping rules and stay limits
  • Vehicle restrictions
  • Land-management notices
  • Emergency alerts

The National Park Service similarly advises visitors to check current conditions and alerts because weather, trails, access, and hazards can change rapidly. https://home.nps.gov/romo/planyourvisit/trail_conditions.htm

You Are Responsible for Your Own Safety

Camping, hiking, overlanding, driving forest roads, and traveling in remote places involve real risks.

These can include becoming stranded, vehicle damage, severe weather, wildfire, flash flooding, falling trees, falling rocks, wildlife, altitude, difficult terrain, lack of cell service, and delayed emergency response.

You are responsible for deciding whether a place, route, activity, or condition is appropriate for you, your vehicle, your equipment, and the people traveling with you.

Public-land safety guidance likewise emphasizes that visitors need to plan for changing conditions and be prepared to turn around when conditions are not safe. https://www.nps.gov/grsm/learn/news/great-smoky-mountains-national-park-urges-visitors-to-plan-and-prepare-following-high-number-of-emergency-incidents-this-march.htm

Road and Vehicle Ratings Are Not Guarantees

Road difficulty, clearance, vehicle suitability, and stress ratings are observations intended to help you understand what a route was like when it was evaluated.

They are not guarantees that your vehicle can safely complete the route.

Vehicle capability depends on many factors, including:

  • Ground clearance
  • Tires
  • Drivetrain
  • Wheelbase
  • Vehicle weight and load
  • Driver experience
  • Weather
  • Mud, snow, rocks, washouts, or erosion
  • Recent road maintenance or damage

If a road looks unsafe when you arrive, do not rely on an old rating just because Llama Scout previously reported it as easy.

Connectivity Can Fail

Cellular and satellite connectivity information can change based on carrier, device, terrain, vegetation, weather, network congestion, satellite visibility, equipment, and changes made by the service provider.

A strong signal observed during one visit does not guarantee service during another.

Do not rely on Llama Scout connectivity ratings as your only emergency communication plan. The National Park Service also warns visitors not to depend solely on cell service in remote areas. Accessibility Information Is Observational

Llama Scout includes accessibility information because details about terrain, parking, walking surfaces, toilets, mobility devices, and step-free access can make a major difference when deciding whether a place is usable.

Those observations are not formal accessibility certifications and do not guarantee that a place will meet a particular person's needs.

Weather, erosion, vegetation, temporary obstacles, maintenance, closures, or changes to facilities can also alter accessibility after a place is scouted.

Sensory Ratings Are Subjective

Noise, privacy, odors, crowds, light, traffic, predictability, and sensory comfort can vary greatly from one person to another.

They can also change by hour, day, season, weather, nearby visitors, generators, wildlife, traffic, and other conditions.

Sensory ratings are intended to give you more information than most outdoor guides provide. They are not promises that you will experience a place the exact same way someone else did.

Coordinates and Maps Can Be Wrong

Coordinates, maps, road names, boundaries, and directions may contain errors or become outdated.

A map showing a road does not necessarily mean that road is open, public, maintained, legal to travel, or suitable for your vehicle.

Respect signs, gates, closures, private property, permits, and instructions from the land manager.

Water and Natural Resources

Streams, springs, lakes, hoses, spigots, or other water sources mentioned on Llama Scout should not automatically be assumed to provide safe drinking water.

Treat or filter natural water appropriately unless an official source specifically identifies it as "potable" drinking water.

Wildlife

Wildlife observations are never guarantees about what animals will or will not be present.

Store food properly, maintain appropriate distance from wildlife, and follow local food-storage and wildlife regulations.

Emergency Situations

Llama Scout is not an emergency service and does not provide emergency monitoring, rescue, dispatch, medical advice, or real-time hazard warnings.

If you are traveling somewhere remote, have an emergency plan before you go. Tell someone where you are going and when you expect to return, and consider communication methods that do not depend entirely on cellular coverage. This is also consistent with current NPS backcountry safety guidance. https://www.nps.gov/articles/hiking-safety.htm

Personally Scouted Does Not Mean Permanently Verified

When Llama Scout marks a location as Llama Scouted, it means the location was personally visited by a Llama Scout team member and observations were recorded.

It does not mean Llama Scout continuously monitors that place or guarantees its current condition.

Check the Scout Report's visit or last-scouted date and consider how much time has passed since that observation.

Use Your Judgment

If what you see in person conflicts with what you read on Llama Scout, trust the conditions in front of you.

Turn around, change plans, or leave whenever conditions do not feel safe or appropriate.

The goal of Llama Scout is to reduce surprises, not pretend the outdoors can ever be completely predictable.
